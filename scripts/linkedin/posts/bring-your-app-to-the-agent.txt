This morning I asked my AI assistant when to go outside for vitamin D. It answered with the synthesis window, the best hour, the minutes I'd need — and I never opened my app. The assistant called it directly, through an MCP server I recently shipped for it.

That small moment is a whole shift in how I think about building software.

For years the question was "how do users interact with my app?" Then, for the last couple of years, "how do I put an agent inside my app?" — the chatbot, the copilot. I still build those for clients.

But there's a third move, and it's the inversion: instead of embedding an agent in your tool, you embed your tool in the agent your user already talks to every day. The app goes inside the assistant. No new interface to adopt, no onboarding — your tool descriptions are the UX now, and the model reads them in milliseconds.

MCP is the current plumbing for it, but the durable shift is the design decision underneath: your app treats its agent-facing interface as a first-class surface. Agent-first is API-first with a new consumer — one that reads docs natively and composes tools on its own.

The bigger prize is on the enterprise side: software that agents can consume gets woven into production processes; software that only humans can consume waits for someone to open a tab.
