You tried ChatGPT a couple of years ago, it made things up, you decided this was overhyped nonsense, and you haven't been back since.

You were right at the time. The tool was worse, the promises were ridiculous, and the people telling you it would change everything couldn't say what it would change for YOU specifically. Forming that opinion was reasonable. It just went out of date.

Here's the part nobody says out loud: the reason you're not using it isn't that you're not technical, and it isn't that you're resistant to change. It's that nobody has ever told you what you can ask it for.

That sounds too simple to be the real problem. It's the whole problem. A model answers what you ask and never mentions what you didn't — so if you think it writes emails and makes things up, you'll ask it to write an email, and it'll do that, and you'll conclude you were right. There is no error message for a question you never thought to ask.

The people in your company getting a lot out of this aren't smarter than you. They stumbled into a wider idea of what's possible, usually by accident, and everything follows from that.

So here's the only suggestion I have, and it takes an hour, once.

Take something you actually did this week — the thing you postponed, the report nobody wants to write, the spreadsheet you fought with, the forty emails you had to summarise for somebody. Not a demo, not a toy. That thing.

Describe it in plain words, the way you'd explain it to a new colleague. Then ask what it would need from you to help, and whether there's a better way to approach it than the one you had in mind.

You are not learning a tool. You're finding out what's in range.

If it's useless for your work, you'll know in an hour and you'll have earned the right to say so — which is more than most people who dismiss it can claim.
