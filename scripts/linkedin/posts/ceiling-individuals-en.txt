You can't ask about a space you don't know is there.

That single sentence explains most of what goes wrong when someone builds software with an agent for the first time.

You don't need to know how authentication works. You need to know that having a login screen and being protected are two different things — because the moment you know that, you can ask, and the agent handles the rest.

If you don't know it, you'll never ask. And the agent won't volunteer it. Agents answer questions well. They're much worse at telling you which question you should have asked.

So the thing standing between you and shipping something real isn't skill. It's a list of categories you've never heard of:

• code can be regenerated, your data can't
• a key you delete from the code is still in the project's history
• by default, nothing caps your bill
• it works with three users and can fall over with three hundred
• "the agent says it works" is not the same as it working

None of those require you to read a line of code. All of them require knowing the category exists.

That's the whole gap. It's smaller and stranger than "learn to code", and nobody teaches it because it falls between the cracks: too basic for engineers, too invisible for everyone else.
