I've published exactly what happens in my workshop. The whole thing — the agenda, what you leave with, what I need from you, and the three rules I don't bend.

That's unusual enough that it's worth explaining why.

If you're buying training, you can't evaluate it before you've bought it. You get a page of outcomes written in the passive voice, some logos, and a promise. Then you spend a day of eight people's time finding out.

So here's mine, in advance, including the parts that don't flatter me.

One of the rules is that your team types. Always. I don't touch your project — not when it's slower, not when the fix is obvious, not when something urgent turns up. If I fix it, you leave with the problem solved and without knowing how to solve it, which is the worse of the two outcomes.

Another is that when nobody in the room can answer a question, that's the result. If it goes quiet at "where are your API keys right now?", I don't rescue it with a generic answer. We write the box down as empty and move on. Knowing a box is empty is the entire point.

And the deliverable ends with a section listing the boxes that are still empty. It's the easiest section to leave out to look good, and the most valuable one — a gap you know about is a managed risk; the dangerous ones are the gaps nobody has named.

None of this is a secret worth keeping. A workshop isn't a script, and reading mine won't let you run it — the same way the system it's built on has been published for weeks and that hasn't made anybody's problems go away.
