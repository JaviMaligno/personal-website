Can you tell where the ball is in a football photo if you erase it? The players give it away — their positions and momentum all point somewhere. It turns out a tiny model can read that signal too.

Trained on nothing but player positions and velocities, a ~60 kB network places a ball it's never shown. And opening it up, what it learned is almost anticlimactic: geometry. Ten interpretable numbers recover almost everything it does, and one dominates — the velocity-weighted centroid, "where the running mass of players is heading." Not a deep pattern; a weighted average.

The map below shows where on the pitch the ball is most inferable from the players (greener = pinned down). Easiest along the touchlines and in open midfield; hardest in the congestion in front of both goals, where bodies pile up and momentum cancels out.

The twist: the model is quietly overconfident on exactly the cases it gets wrong — a ball loose in open space, far from everyone. No collective motion to read, no signal — and it doesn't know it's guessing.
