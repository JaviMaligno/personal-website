Three questions. No wrong answers, but there are answers most people get wrong.

1. You delete an API key from your code and save the change. Is it still exposed?

2. Your app has a login screen. Can anyone read the data without going through it?

3. Nobody uses your app for a whole month. What does it cost you?

If you hesitated on any of them, that hesitation is the whole point — and it isn't a gap in your coding.

The first one: yes, it's still there, sitting in the project's history. Deleting isn't enough; the key has to be replaced.

The second: yes, if the part that serves the data never checks who's asking. A login screen protects the screen.

The third: whatever gets charged regardless of use. And a busy month has no ceiling unless you set one.

None of those require reading a line of code. All of them require knowing the category exists — because an agent will answer any question you ask, and won't tell you which one you should have asked.
