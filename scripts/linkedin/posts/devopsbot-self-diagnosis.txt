My DevOps agent found its own bug.

Someone asked it to check resource usage for a service. The Grafana query was failing. The bot investigated — and traced the error back to its own code.

ValueError: invalid literal for int() with base 10: '1h'

Its Grafana tool was passing step: '1h' as a string when the implementation expected an integer. The agent identified the root cause, reported it ("the error was mine"), and then did something I didn't expect:

Instead of stopping, it pivoted. Checked what it could verify — deployment status, replicas, pod health, resource definitions. Gave an honest answer: "here's what I can confirm, here's what I can't yet, and here's how to fix it."

No hallucinated numbers. No pretending everything was fine.

This is the kind of behavior that builds trust with a team. Not perfection — transparency. An agent that fails honestly is more useful than one that guesses confidently.

We launched this read-only agent in our #devops_requests channel this week. It's already handling ~70% of infrastructure questions autonomously. The roadmap: safe actions in dev/test next, then approval workflows for production.
