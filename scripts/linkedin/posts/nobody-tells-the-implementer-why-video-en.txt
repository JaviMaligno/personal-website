Nobody tells the implementer why.

I passed a client's request down a team of agents, from the lead to whoever writes the code. Names, numbers and prohibitions almost all arrived. For two hops, so did the reasons.

Then the architect wrote to the implementer, and the "because" survived in only 8–31% of those messages. It isn't worn away, like in the telephone game. It's cut. Even the lead, writing that message itself with the client's request in hand, kept the reason only 24% of the time.

Whether losing it hurts the code is less tidy than I expected. But if the why matters to whoever builds, put it in their message yourself, or attach the original.
