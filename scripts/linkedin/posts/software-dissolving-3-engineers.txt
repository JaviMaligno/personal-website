A friend told me he'd been trying to get an LLM to write internal reports following his company's exact style guidelines.

After enough iteration, he realized the prompt-engineering effort was taking longer than writing the reports himself.

Then he said something that stuck with me:

"Maybe the problem isn't that the AI can't adapt. Maybe we're the ones who should adapt. We're making it less efficient with all our constraints, when what it produces on its own is good enough for the purpose."

That insight isn't about reports. It's about the entire software stack right now.

A markdown skill is what you get when you stop forcing the model into the shape of a typed SDK. Generative UI is what you get when you stop forcing it to emit valid React components.

The friction we were adding — typed APIs, deterministic UIs, hand-coded glue — was buying us correctness, but at a cost we hadn't priced in. Many of those constraints existed because humans needed them, not because the model did.

For engineers, the corollary is uncomfortable: when the middle layer gets thin, you can't hide in a development niche and still add value. The model renders the surface. The agent carries the plumbing. What's left looks more like product judgment than implementation.

The part you used to be paid for — the implementation — is the part that's compressing.
