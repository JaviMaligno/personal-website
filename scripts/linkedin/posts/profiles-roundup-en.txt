Everyone building software with agents is making a mistake right now. It just isn't the same mistake.

If you have no technical background, you're delegating ABOVE your level. You hand over decisions you have no way to check — and worse, you don't know they were decisions. The agent chose how to store your data, what's public, what happens when something fails. From your side, none of that looked like anything happening at all.

If you came from engineering, you're delegating BELOW yours. You review every line by eye, break work into pieces small enough to inspect, and give instructions more precise than the job needs. You already know verification by eyeball doesn't scale — you spent a career building the machinery that replaced it — and you're not using any of it here.

If you came from WordPress, no-code or automations, you're closer than you think. You already know a site can be live or not live, that a plugin can break everything, that some things live in the tool and some in your account. Same categories, learned in a different shape. What's missing is narrower: those platforms had walls, and now there aren't any.

What all three have in common: doing what previous experience says to do. One has no reflex so hands over everything. One has a strong reflex so hands over as little as possible. Neither has adjusted to the tool.

And only one of the two failure modes looks like diligence, which is what makes it the harder one to fix. Nobody is ever going to take you aside and suggest you review less.

The destination is the same regardless of where you started: holding the mechanism instead of holding the work.
