I pasted the wrong thing into 24 conversations on purpose and read every reply by hand.

Eight unrelated topics, a house move, a 10K plan, an electricity bill, and then a block of text dropped in raw, the way a real misfire arrives: a stack trace, a recipe, a job ad, a prompt from another chat. Three models: GPT-5.6 sol, GPT-5.6 luna and Claude Opus 5.

Not one reply asked whether the paste was meant for that conversation. Zero out of 24.

What varied was how much unrequested work they did. Fourteen simply took the paste as the new task. Five asked a clarifying question, about the output format, never about whether it belonged. One invented a plausible bridge between a night-shift job ad and a 10K training plan. Another took on the role of a pasted style-editor prompt and applied it to its own earlier answers.

One reply got it right: it noticed the paste did not belong, considered whether there was a real link, and rejected it with a reason. So the ceiling exists. This is not a capability problem.

It is 24 conversations, an observation rather than a measurement. The next article runs a proper grid.
