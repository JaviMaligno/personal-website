Karpathy describes LLM adoption as a funnel: most people have barely touched these tools, ~20% use them as a better Google, and a tiny sliver watch months-long projects get done in days.

The same funnel shows up inside any company that buys licences for everyone: a few people use it daily, some occasionally and badly, and many never open it.

His first factor is the key: impact scales with problem size. And in a business team, what caps problem size usually isn't ambition. It's that the person is the cable: copy, paste into the chat, carry the answer back by hand. That only fits a paragraph-sized problem.

Those people don't lack an explanation. Karpathy says it too: they understand it, just second-hand. What they lack is doing one real task of their own with the tool connected to their work, and knowing what to ask once what they build starts to matter.
