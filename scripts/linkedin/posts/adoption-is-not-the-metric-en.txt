Every AI rollout I hear about produces the same shape. A small group uses it every day and gets a lot out of it. Some use it occasionally and badly. A large share never opened it at all.

Licences get bought, dashboards show adoption, and the organisation moves at exactly the speed it did before. Both things are true at once, which is why the meeting about it goes nowhere.

I agree with that diagnosis. What I don't agree with is the conclusion people draw from it — that half the organisation is never getting there.

That conclusion assumes the distance is general ability. It isn't. Watch what actually separates the person who gets a lot from the person who gets almost nothing, and it isn't talent or technical background. It's that one of them knows what can be asked for and the other doesn't.

Which is also why the two failing groups are failing for the same reason. The person who uses it badly asks for something small and takes whatever comes back. The person who never opened it doesn't ask at all. Neither of them has any idea of the actual range, and nothing corrects them, because a model answers what you ask and never mentions what you didn't.

There's no error message for a question you never asked. That's the whole gap, and it's a short list of concrete things rather than a career's worth of skill.

I don't think you close it with a rollout. Access was never the constraint — the licences are already there, unopened. You close it by showing people what's askable, using their own work, until they've done one useful thing with it themselves.

The pessimistic read treats "hasn't happened" as "can't happen". Those aren't the same, and the difference is worth a lot of money to whoever gets it right.
